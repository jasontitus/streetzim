# Proposal: Kiwix builds a map's language versions from a recipe

Status: proposal, 2026-10-23, for the Kiwix product lead and the openZIM
developers. Nothing in Kiwix, libkiwix or Zimfarm depends on it yet. This
is option A; options B and C are under [Alternatives](#alternatives).
Numbers marked *estimate* have not been measured.

## The ask

StreetZim publishes one map per region (OpenStreetMap vector tiles,
search, routing, terrain, Wikipedia articles about the region's places).
A visitor in another language wants the same map with the Wikipedia
articles, the place names and Kiwix's own search in their language. Today
StreetZim makes that file without storing it, as a **language version**:

- the region's **base** ZIM, which every visitor downloads anyway;
- a few **packs**, small ZIMs with the Wikipedia articles in one language,
  and a **search pack** with libzim's title and full-text indexes for that
  combination;
- a **recipe** (`streetzim-recipe/2`, JSON) that lists the language
  version's bytes in order: ranges of the base, ranges of the packs and a
  few MB of generated bytes, with a SHA-256 for every 16 MiB of every
  input and the MD5 of the result.

Splicing the ranges in order gives a normal ZIM, byte for byte the file
StreetZim merged and checked when it wrote the recipe (zimcheck,
kiwix-serve `/suggest` and `/search`, the viewer). The website's builder
page does this in the browser today (`web/build/`).

**We propose that libkiwix learn to do the same:** a catalog entry for a
language version points to its recipe; the Kiwix apps download the inputs
they do not have from the usual mirrors, splice them, check the result and
add it to the library as an ordinary book. A visitor who already has the
base downloads only the packs.

## Why

**What is stored.** Measured on the language versions published for four
regions (October 2026; every version a full ZIM of its own versus the
parts):

| region | base | versions | each version | all versions as files | base + packs + search packs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Switzerland | 2.28 GB | 23 | 3.03-3.12 GB | 70.4 GB | about 7.2 GB |
| Baltics | 0.98 GB | 22 | 1.27-1.32 GB | 28.2 GB | about 4.0 GB |
| Greece | 1.05 GB | 22 | 1.16-1.21 GB | 25.8 GB | about 2.4 GB |
| Iceland | 0.33 GB | 19 | 0.35-0.36 GB | 6.7 GB | about 0.8 GB |

(The last column adds each pack once, plus one search pack per distinct
pack set, from the download sizes the site lists.) Full files per
language take about ten times what the parts take, on the mirrors and in
Zimfarm's uploads; the packs and search packs together weigh one to
three bases.

**What a visitor downloads.** With the Swiss base already on the device, a
language version needs 0.74-0.84 GB of packs instead of 3.03-3.12 GB.
Without it, the same 3.0-3.1 GB as a prebuilt file.

**What the language changes.** The base already carries labels in 21
languages plus the region's own (`name:XX` in the tiles), search by those
names, and a viewer in 22 interface languages ([zimfarm.md, "Multilingual
maps"](zimfarm.md#multilingual-maps---multilingual)). What is per language
by nature is the Wikipedia text and Kiwix's own search indexes, which a
ZIM can hold only once ([language-packs.md](language-packs.md)). That is
what the packs carry.

## What a recipe is

`tools/langpack/recipe2.py` writes recipes; `tools/langpack/merge_zim.py`
merges the same files into a real ZIM from the same byte stream, so the two
cannot disagree. The interface is in
[language-matrix-prototype.md](language-matrix-prototype.md), "Recipes";
the operator's guide is [language-pack-pipeline.md](language-pack-pipeline.md).

```json
{"format": "streetzim-recipe/2", "uuid": "...", "name": "osm_de_switzerland",
 "title": "Schweiz", "language": "deu", "flavour": "maxi_wiki-en-de-fr-it-rm",
 "size": 3025954350, "md5": "<the ZIM's own checksum>", "file_md5": "<md5sum of the file>",
 "base": {"file": "osm-switzerland-2026-10-22.zim", "size": 2283524130, "uuid": "...",
          "range": [65616, 2283524114], "chunk": 16777216, "sha256": ["...137 hashes..."],
          "mask": [[777355760, 1048576], "..."], "slots": ["..."]},
 "packs": {"de": {"file": "osm_switzerland_wiki-de_2026-10-22_c6304e7e.zim", "url": "...",
                  "size": 229454627, "chunk": 16777216, "sha256": ["..."]},
           "search": {"kind": "search", "...": "..."}},
 "segments": [{"src": "inline", "len": 65616, "data": "<base64>", "sha256": "..."},
              {"src": "base", "off": 65616, "len": 777290144},
              {"src": "pack", "pack": "de", "off": 65616, "len": 225997506},
              "...",
              {"src": "md5"}]}
```

- **Segments.** The result is the concatenation of the segments: `inline`
  bytes (base64, each with its SHA-256), ranges of the base or of a pack,
  and the 16-byte MD5 at the end. The Swiss German recipe has 1,997
  segments (the English and Romansh ones, with the same packs, too).
  Eight ranges of 1 MiB or more carry 99.6% of the bytes: the
  base on either side of its viewer files, the clusters of the four
  Wikipedia packs (de, fr, it, rm) and of the search pack, and one 4 MB
  run of the base's own tables. The rest is
  the generated head (header and MIME list, 64 KB), the base's three
  viewer files (1.4 MB, see below) and the generated tables at the end
  (pointer lists, the title listing, the packs' directory entries),
  interleaved with short base ranges. A first prototype with no viewer
  slots needed 23-34 segments per file.
- **Layout.** The base's clusters and directory entries stay at their own
  offsets, so the language version starts with the base's bytes. Pack
  paths (`~w/<lang>/...`) sort after every base path; a pack adds entries
  and never replaces or deletes one. `map-config.json` and the metadata
  (Name, Title, Language, Flavour, Description) are generated.
- **Checks.** Every input is hashed in 16 MiB chunks from offset 0, so a
  reader can check any range it receives against `sha256` before writing
  it. Inline bytes carry their own SHA-256. The output's MD5 (the ZIM
  checksum libzim verifies) and `file_md5` are compared at the end.
- **UUID.** `derive_uuid` in `merge_zim.py`: a UUIDv5 of the base UUID,
  the pack UUIDs, the language, the merger version, a hash of the
  generated `map-config.json` and metadata, and the inputs' MD5s. The same
  inputs always give the same UUID; any change gives a new one, which
  Kiwix needs because it dedupes books by UUID.
- **Viewer slots.** StreetZim patches a published base's viewer in place,
  in fixed-size uncompressed slots (`cloud/patch_viewer_inplace.py`),
  keeping its size and UUID. A recipe carries the slots inline and lists
  them, with the base's checksum, in `base.mask`; the base's chunk hashes
  are taken with those bytes zeroed. A local base one viewer patch older
  or newer still assembles the same file.
- **Size.** Switzerland's recipes are 13.7-15.5 MB of JSON, almost all of
  it the base64 inline bytes (10.1 MB raw); German is 3.7 MB gzipped.
  Greece's are 4.8-5.8 MB, Iceland's 2.2-2.3 MB. They grow with the packs'
  entries, not with the base.

## How it would fit Kiwix

This section is our reading of libkiwix and the apps from the outside; the
Kiwix developers will know better where each piece belongs.

### Catalog (OPDS)

A language version is an OPDS entry like any book: its own UUID, Name
(`osm_<lang>_<region>`), Title and Language in that language, Flavour,
size, the base's illustration. Its acquisition links:

- a **recipe link**, with a new media type (for example
  `application/vnd.openzim.recipe+json`), for apps that can assemble;
- a **plain link** that older apps can use unchanged: the language
  version streamed by a relay (option B, which exists today, see below),
  or a torrent with that relay as webseed.

An app that does not know the recipe type ignores that link and offers
the plain download, so nothing changes for it.

The catalog needs fields a recipe does not carry yet: article and media
counts, tags, the illustration's URL. The recipe writer can add them; the
merger already computes the counts.

The packs and search packs are ZIM files, but useless alone (their
articles link back into the base). They should be hidden from the catalog
while still on the mirrors.

### Download and assembly

The simplest integration reuses the download manager as it is:

1. Fetch the recipe (Switzerland: 14-16 MB of JSON, about 4 MB gzipped)
   and check its format and bounds: every segment inside its input, the
   lengths adding up to `size`.
2. Look for the base in the library by the recipe's `base.uuid`; if
   present, hash its 16 MiB chunks with the mask zeroed and compare (one
   read of the base: *estimate*, seconds per GB on a desktop, longer on a
   phone).
3. Download what is missing, base and packs, as ordinary files, from the
   mirrors and with the same metalinks and checksums as any ZIM. Pack
   ranges cover 85-99% of each pack file, so whole-file downloads waste
   little.
4. Splice the segments into the output in order, checking each chunk and
   each inline piece, computing the MD5 as the bytes go out, and compare
   it with `md5` and `file_md5`. Memory stays constant (one chunk).
5. Add the result to the library; delete the packs; keep or delete the
   base as the user chooses.

The splicer is small: the browser's (`web/build/assembler-worker.js`,
about 500 lines of plain JavaScript, plus `fetcher.js` for the ranges)
and the Python one (`recipe2.py materialize`) show the whole algorithm. A C++
version in libkiwix would be *estimate*: 300-500 lines plus tests.

A later version could ask for byte ranges directly (HTTP ranges over the
mirrors, checked against the chunk hashes like metalink pieces) and write
the output while downloading, as the builder page does. That saves the
packs' temporary copy but needs range support in the download manager.

### Verification and resume

- Every byte from a mirror is checked against the recipe's SHA-256 before
  it is written; a bad mirror costs one chunk, fetched again elsewhere.
- The result passes libzim's own checksum (`Archive::check()`), since its
  MD5 is the recipe's.
- Downloads of the inputs resume as today. The splice step restarts from
  the last written chunk boundary: rehashing the part already written for
  the MD5 runs at disk speed (Switzerland's 3 GB: about 4 s at the
  0.8 GB/s the merger measured on StreetZim's build host).

### Storage

- Peak during assembly: base + packs + output. Switzerland: 2.28 + 0.77 +
  3.03 = about 6.1 GB.
- The output's first 2.28 GB are the base's bytes at the same offsets
  except the head (64 KB) and the viewer slots (1.4 MB). So an app that
  does not keep the base can turn the base file into the language version
  in place (rewrite those bytes, truncate at `base.range[1]`, append the
  rest): peak output + packs, about 3.8 GB. On copy-on-write filesystems
  (APFS on iOS and macOS, btrfs, XFS with reflinks) a clone of the base
  keeps both files for the cost of one plus the packs. *Not implemented
  anywhere yet.*

### Updates

- A rebuilt base has a new UUID, so every recipe changes. A rebuilt pack,
  or a viewer patch of the base, changes the inputs' MD5s and so the
  language version's UUID too. Each language version keeps its Name, so
  the apps' "update available" logic by Name and date works unchanged.
- An update downloads the changed inputs only. A rebuilt pack: that pack
  and the new search pack. A viewer patch: nothing but the recipe, since
  the new viewer files come inline and the local base still matches the
  masked chunk hashes. A rebuilt base: the base and every pack, as today.

### Older apps

They see the plain link and download the whole language version from the
relay or by torrent, as they download any ZIM. They do not reuse a base.
The file they get is the same bytes, with the same UUID and MD5.

## What Zimfarm would build

| output | tool | on Zimfarm |
|---|---|---|
| the base | `streetzim --multilingual --region-languages ...` | buildable now ([zimfarm.md](zimfarm.md#multilingual-maps---multilingual)) |
| a Wikipedia pack per (region, language) | `tools/build_wiki_pack.py` (`streetzim/wiki_pack.py`) | needs its own offliner or mode; reads a whole local Kiwix Wikipedia ZIM of that language (a download per task: [zimfarm.md](zimfarm.md#wikipedia-articles-on-zimfarm-the-api-not-a-wikipedia-zim) on why the base uses the API instead) plus that language's `page_props` and `page` dumps for the Q-ID to title lookup, or would need an API mode like `--wikipedia`'s |
| a search pack per pack set | `tools/langpack/search_packs.py`, `index_pack.py` (libzim's writer) | runs anywhere the base and packs are |
| recipes and their checks | `recipe2.py`, `tools/langpack/run_gates.sh` (merge, `verify_merge.py`, zimcheck, kiwix-serve, the viewer in Chromium) | the merge, verify and zimcheck steps fit a task; the browser check needs Chromium, not in the image |

A `streetzim-langpack` offliner (one task per region: packs, search packs,
recipes, checks) is not written yet. Publishing needs openZIM's decisions:
where recipes live in the warehouse, how the catalog generator turns them
into entries, and how packs stay off the catalog.

On StreetZim's own host, a region's packs, search packs, recipes and
checks take hours (Switzerland, 23 versions: 6.75 h, of which about 2 h
search packs and 3.75 h checks; Greece, 22 versions: about 2 h end to end,
[language-pack-pipeline.md](language-pack-pipeline.md), "Sizes").

## Alternatives

### B. A stateless relay serves each language version as a file

StreetZim runs one today: `build-proxy/`, a Cloudflare Worker on the free
plan, streams any language version from the base and pack ranges on
archive.org plus small blobs, with HTTP ranges and a known length; it is
also the webseed of a torrent per language version
([build-proxy/README.md](../build-proxy/README.md)). Nothing is stored and
nothing is computed but the splice.

- **For:** no change in Kiwix; the catalog lists a URL and an MD5 (known
  in advance from the recipe). It is the plain link of option A.
- **Against:** every visitor downloads the whole version, base included;
  the relay is a service someone runs and pays for (the free plan allows
  100,000 requests a day per account; 50 subrequests per request, so a
  version is served in ranges, and a whole-file GET of a large one is
  refused); its throughput is archive.org's per connection; mirrors could
  serve the parts but not the virtual file unless each runs the relay.

### C. One multilingual ZIM and a language switch in the viewer

The base already switches the map's labels, its search names and the
interface to the visitor's language. Adding every language's Wikipedia
and one search index would make a single file for everyone.

- **For:** no assembly, no recipes, no catalog change; one file per region.
- **Against:** every visitor downloads every language's Wikipedia:
  Switzerland's 22 packs are 1.27 GB, so the file would be about 3.6 GB
  plus a combined index (*estimate*) against 3.03-3.12 GB for one
  version; larger regions grow faster. Kiwix's search has one index of
  each kind per ZIM with one stemmer, so search in most languages would be
  worse; the ZIM has one Title and one `Language` for the catalog.

C is half done and stays useful: the base is a good map in any of the 22
languages without packs. A and B add the per-language Wikipedia and
search; A lets the apps reuse what the user already has, B keeps older
apps served.

## Security and integrity

- **The recipe is the trust anchor.** Whoever writes the recipe decides
  the bytes, inline viewer code included, exactly as whoever publishes a
  ZIM decides its contents. It must come from the catalog over HTTPS,
  like the checksums the catalog gives today. Signing recipes (and
  catalogs) is an open question, not a new requirement.
- **Mirrors and relays are untrusted.** Every range is checked against
  the recipe's chunk SHA-256 before it is written; the output is checked
  against the MD5. A mirror can make a download fail, not change it.
- **Parsing.** A reader should refuse a recipe over a size limit (the
  site's is 32 MiB), an unknown format, a segment outside its input or
  lengths that do not add up to `size`, and an input file name with a
  path in it. Output paths are the app's, never the recipe's.
- **Resources.** Constant memory; disk checked against `size` before
  starting.
- **Privacy.** The apps fetch the same kinds of files from the same
  mirrors as today; no new server sees what a user assembles.

## Effort and milestones

All durations are *estimates* for one developer who knows the code base.

1. **Spec and test vectors** (1-2 weeks): freeze the format under an
   openZIM name, a JSON schema, small test files (a base, two packs, a
   search pack, their recipe, the expected MD5), and the catalog fields.
   StreetZim side.
2. **libkiwix** (2-4 weeks): the recipe reader and splicer with resume,
   the base lookup, and the hook into the download manager.
3. **Catalog** (1-2 weeks): the OPDS link type and fields, `library.xml`
   support, hiding packs; the catalog generator reading recipes.
4. **Apps** (2-4 weeks each): Desktop, Android, iOS: the language menu on
   a book, progress through the splice step, the keep-the-base choice.
5. **Zimfarm** (2-3 weeks): the `streetzim-langpack` offliner and
   publishing recipes and packs.

What exists today: the format, the merger and its checks, recipes for
four regions, the browser assembler (`web/build/`), the relay and the
torrents. The browser assembles a Swiss version in 10-26 minutes in our
runs, depending on the connection and on whether the base is already on
the device.

## Open questions

1. **Who owns the format?** A StreetZim-only format, or an openZIM one
   ("a ZIM defined as ranges of other ZIMs") that other scrapers could use
   for their own per-language variants.
2. **Recipe size.** 13.7-15.5 MB of JSON for Switzerland (3.7 MB gzipped).
   Binary inline bytes beside the JSON would remove the base64 overhead;
   the viewer slots are the same for every version of a region and could
   be shared.
3. **Packs in the catalog:** hidden entirely, or listed as add-ons of the
   base?
4. **Keeping the base:** keep both files, transform in place, or clone on
   copy-on-write filesystems; and what the library shows for each.
5. **Whole files or ranges** for the first libkiwix version.
6. **The fallback link:** who runs the relay for older apps, or whether
   torrents with a mirror webseed are enough.
7. **Signing** recipes or the catalog.
8. **Which languages** get versions per region: StreetZim publishes the
   21 label languages plus English and the region's own that have a Kiwix
   Wikipedia ZIM (`CORE_LANGS` in `tools/langpack/region_meta.py`).
